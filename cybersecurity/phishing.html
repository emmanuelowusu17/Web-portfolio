Spot Phishing

Learn the warning signs of fake emails and texts before they trick you.

What Is Phishing?

Phishing is when scammers send emails or text messages that pretend to be from a company you know, like your bank, a delivery service, or a streaming app. Their goal is to trick you into clicking a harmful link, opening an attachment, or giving away your passwords or financial information.

Common stories scammers use

"We noticed suspicious activity or a log-in attempt on your account."
"There's a problem with your account or payment information."
"Please confirm your personal or financial information."
An invoice for something you never bought.
"You're eligible for a government refund."
A coupon or offer for free stuff.

Can You Spot the Red Flags?

Practice example: This is a fake email made up for learning. "StreamFlix" is not a real company.

1 From: StreamFlix Billing <billing@streamfIix-support.net>

2 Subject: URGENT: Your account will be suspended in 24 hours

3 Dear Customer,

We were unable to process your last payment. Your account is now on hold. 4 To avoid losing access, please update your payment details within 24 hours.

Update Payment Now 5

Link goes to: http://streamflix.account-billing-verify.net/login

Thank you,
The StreamFlix Team

  1. Wrong sender address. Look closely: "streamfIix" uses a capital I instead of a lowercase l, and it isn't the company's real website.
  2. Urgent threat. Scammers rush you with scary deadlines so you act before you think.
  3. Generic greeting. "Dear Customer" instead of your name.
  4. Payment problem story. Real companies won't email or text you a link to update your payment information.
  5. Suspicious link. The button leads to a strange website, not the company's real one.
Good grammar doesn't mean it's safe. Bad spelling used to be a clue, but scammers can now use AI to write messages with perfect grammar.

Recognize. Resist. Delete.

🔍

1. Recognize

Watch for urgent language, requests for personal info, misspelled addresses or links, and shortened links you don't trust.

✋

2. Resist

Don't click links or open attachments. If the message might be real, go to the company's website or app yourself instead.

🗑️

3. Report & Delete

Report the message, then delete it. Don't reply, and don't click "unsubscribe."

What If...?

Don't use any link or phone number in the message. Contact the company using a website, app, or phone number you already know is real, like the one on your card or your last bill.

Update your security software, run a scan, and remove anything it finds. If you typed a password on the page, change that password right away, and turn on MFA for that account.

Go to IdentityTheft.gov. It gives you step-by-step actions based on what was shared, like your Social Security, credit card, or bank account number.

  • Email: forward it to reportphishing@apwg.org
  • Text message: forward it to SPAM (7726)
  • Any scam: report it to the FTC at ReportFraud.ftc.gov
