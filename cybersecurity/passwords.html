Create Strong Passwords

A strong password is your first lock against criminals. Here's how to make one you can actually remember.

The 3 Rules of a Strong Password

📏

Long

Use at least 16 characters. Longer is stronger!

🎲

Random

Use a random mix of characters, or a passphrase of unrelated words. Never your name, birthday, or pet's name.

🧩

Unique

Use a different password for every account. If one gets stolen, the others stay safe.

How to Make a Passphrase

  1. Pick 4 to 7 words that have nothing to do with each other.
  2. Don't use song lyrics, quotes, or famous phrases.
  3. Picture a silly scene with the words to help you remember it.
  4. Spaces between the words are optional.

Use a Password Manager

A password manager is an app that creates, saves, and fills in strong passwords for all your accounts. You only have to remember one strong password: the one that unlocks the manager.

Many phones and web browsers already have one built in.

Password Myths vs. Facts

NIST writes the official U.S. rules that websites follow for passwords. Here's what those rules say about some common beliefs.

Myth: Symbols and numbers matter more than length.

Fact: Length matters most. NIST says websites should not force you to mix in symbols, numbers, or capital letters.

Myth: You should change your password every few months.

Fact: NIST says websites should not force regular changes. Change your password when there's a sign it was stolen.

Myth: Password hints and security questions keep you safe.

Fact: Answers like your first pet's name are easy to guess or find. NIST says websites should not use them.

Myth: A common password is fine if the site accepts it.

Fact: NIST says websites must check new passwords against a list of common and stolen passwords and reject them.
