Turn On MFA

Multifactor authentication (MFA) adds a second check when you log in, so a stolen password isn't enough to get into your account.

What Is MFA?

MFA means using more than a password to log in. It asks for two or more of these types of proof that you are really you:

🧠

Something you know

Your password or PIN.

📱

Something you have

Your phone, an authenticator app, or a security key.

👆

Something you are

Your fingerprint or face.

How MFA Stops Criminals

🕵️

A criminal steals your password from a phishing email.

→ ↓
🔐

They try to log in, but the account asks for a second step.

→ ↓
🛡️

They don't have your phone or fingerprint, so they're blocked.

Which MFA Method Is Best?

Any MFA is much safer than a password alone. But if an account gives you a choice, pick the strongest one.

Strongest

Passkey or security key

You log in with your phone, fingerprint, face, or a small physical key, with no code to type. NIST calls this kind of login phishing-resistant: it won't work on a fake website, even if you're tricked into visiting one.

Strong

Authenticator app

An app on your phone creates a new code every 30 seconds. It's a big step up from a password alone, but a fake website could still trick you into typing the code in.

Better than nothing

Text message or email code

A code is sent to your phone number or email. NIST restricts codes sent to phone numbers, because criminals can sometimes move your number to their own phone. Use this only if the account offers nothing stronger.

Never share an MFA code. If someone calls, texts, or emails asking for the code you just received, it's a scam. Only type codes into a website or app you opened yourself.

Turn It On Here First

  1. Email: it can be used to reset the passwords for your other accounts.
  2. Bank and payment apps
  3. Healthcare accounts
  4. School and work accounts
  5. Social media

Then turn it on for every account or app that offers it.

How to Turn It On

  1. Open the account's Settings. Look under Profile, Privacy, or Security.
  2. Find the MFA option. It may be called two-factor authentication, 2FA, or two-step verification.
  3. Choose a method, using the strongest one offered, and follow the steps.
